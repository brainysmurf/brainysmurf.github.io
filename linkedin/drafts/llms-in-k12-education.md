The tech press has spent two years covering AI through the lens of enterprise productivity.

Meanwhile, the most transformative application of LLMs is happening in classrooms with almost zero mainstream attention.

I'm not talking about students cheating with ChatGPT. I'm talking about what happens when teachers — the most chronically under-resourced professionals in any developed economy — get access to a tool that turns hours of work into minutes.

What I'm seeing from inside edtech:

📝 Report writing — An IB teacher writing reports for 120 students used to lose an entire weekend. With LLMs, the quality is genuinely good when prompted well.

📚 Differentiation — Creating five levels of the same activity used to be impossible. Now it's feasible.

✅ Assessment design — Writing questions that align to specific learning objectives, avoid bias, and span cognitive levels. LLMs are well-suited for this.

Why isn't anyone paying attention?

1. Education moves in academic years, not quarters
2. The "cheating" narrative consumed all the oxygen
3. There's no VC-scale money in it

But here's the math: 70 million teachers worldwide. 1.5 billion students. Even marginal teacher productivity gains compound into something enormous.

And unlike enterprise gains that accrue to shareholders, teacher productivity gains flow directly to students. A teacher who spends less time on admin spends more time doing what only humans can do: building relationships.

This is the most underrated story in tech right now. If you're building in this space, pay attention.
