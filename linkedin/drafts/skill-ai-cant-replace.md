I've used AI coding tools daily for over a year. Copilot, Claude Code, Cursor — the full rotation.

When people ask "what should I learn that AI won't replace?" I have a specific answer.

It's not algorithms. It's not system design. It's not "soft skills" in the vague way people usually mean that.

It's judgment about what to build.

AI is excellent at:
→ Implementing well-defined specs
→ Debugging when you describe symptoms
→ Generating boilerplate and tests
→ Translating between languages

AI consistently struggles with:
→ Deciding which feature actually matters to users
→ Knowing when a "quick fix" is creating future debt
→ Reading team dynamics that affect what ships
→ Recognising when the stated problem isn't the real problem

Last quarter my team chose between a technically elegant solution (Option A) and a boring simple one (Option B).

We shipped Option B.

Why? The roadmap was about to change. Option A would've taken 3x longer. A school was starting their academic year. And our support team could debug Option B at 2am.

None of those inputs were technical. All were critical.

My advice for developers:

Stop optimising for code output — that's being commoditised.
Start optimising for judgment — understand users, business, and tradeoffs.
Learn to write a good brief — it's the highest-leverage AI skill.
Embrace the "boring" parts of seniority — mentoring, architecture, stakeholder management.

The question "should we build this, given everything we know?" requires wisdom from experience.

That's the skill to invest in.
